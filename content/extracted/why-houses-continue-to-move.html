Houses keep moving after the keys are handed over. That is not automatically a defect. Wood dries. Concrete shrinks. Soil under a new load compresses. Seasons change the moisture in the ground and in the framing.

This page explains why movement can continue and when “it is still moving” is the wrong panic.

Movement that is ordinary

  • Early settlement in the first years
  • Seasonal swell and shrink in expansive soil
  • Truss lift and drywall cracks that open and close
  • Finish materials reporting small framing change

Movement that is not ordinary

  • Change that is one-way and still accelerating
  • One corner or side dropping while the rest is quiet
  • A moisture source that was never fixed

Related dictionary terms

  • Settlement
  • Differential Settlement
  • Expansive Soil
  • Moisture Content
  • Settlement Monitoring

(Each of these terms is explained in the MFRC Foundation Dictionary.)

A final note

This page is educational, not diagnostic. “The house moved” is a starting sentence. “Is it still moving, and why?” is the useful one.
