Stucco is a brittle skin on a house that still moves with heat, humidity, and framing. That is why a stable home in a dry climate can show a map of hairline cracks and still be doing its job.

Why stucco cracks without a foundation story

  • The coat shrinks as it dries
  • The wood behind it swells and shrinks
  • Openings concentrate stress at corners
  • A dry climate speeds the first-year cracking

Stucco is applied wet and cures by drying, which means it is shrinking from day one — the same volume-change process that cracks concrete slabs, just on a thinner, more brittle skin. A hot, dry climate accelerates that drying, so the first-year cracking that would take longer elsewhere can show up fast here.

What those cracks do not automatically mean

  • The house is failing
  • Moisture is coming through every line
  • The slab has lost bearing

When stucco is not the whole story

  • A crack is wide, offset, or still growing
  • It matches a pattern through masonry or a foundation wall
  • Water is using the crack and the source is not a surface coat issue

How MFRC suggests homeowners think about it

Instead of asking "why does my stucco have cracks," ask "did this crack appear in the first year or two and stay the same size since, or is it still opening?" Stucco that cracked once while curing and has been quiet since is describing the coat. Stucco that keeps widening years into the life of the house is describing something else.

A final note

This page is educational, not diagnostic. Stucco is often the first finish to complain. It is a poor stand-in for a soil diagnosis.
