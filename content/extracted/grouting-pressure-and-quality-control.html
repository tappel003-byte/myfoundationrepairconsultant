A micropile design assumes certain things happened during construction — a certain grout volume, a certain bond zone, a certain installation method. Quality control is how anyone finds out whether those assumptions actually held.

This page explains what real QC actually checks, and why a single number — pressure, or volume — rarely tells the whole story on its own.

What quality control is actually trying to establish

Installation records — drilling depth, grout mix, grout volume, grout pressure, and how those change with depth — exist to answer one question: did construction actually match what the design assumed? Without that documentation, an installed micropile's real capacity is uncertain even if it looks complete from the surface.

Why volume matters — in both directions

Substantially less grout than the borehole's volume would predict raises real questions: incomplete filling, voids, or a measurement problem. Substantially more can point the other way: an enlarged borehole, fractures, or grout loss into ground that wasn't accounted for. Neither reading, by itself, diagnoses the problem — but both tell an engineer that something happened that deserves a closer look.

Why pressure has to be read in context, not as a standalone score

Pressure changes with depth, ground type, and grouting method — it's diagnostic information, not a universal measure of quality. High pressure doesn't automatically mean a better pile, and low pressure doesn't automatically mean a worse one. What matters is whether the pressure response makes sense for the specific method and ground being worked in.

Common misconceptions

  • "More grout volume always means more capacity." Sometimes it reflects beneficial ground treatment. Sometimes it means grout loss into a void. The number alone doesn't say which.
  • "High grouting pressure proves a stronger pile." Pressure has to be interpreted alongside volume and the ground conditions being worked in — not read on its own.
  • "Good grout-cube strength equals a good pile." It confirms the grout itself met spec. It doesn't confirm the grout-to-ground bond, which is a separate question entirely.

How MFRC suggests homeowners think about it

Instead of asking "was the pressure high enough," ask "is there a real installation record showing what actually happened at each pile, not just a single number after the fact?" For work below grade, that record is often the only lasting evidence of what's actually there.
