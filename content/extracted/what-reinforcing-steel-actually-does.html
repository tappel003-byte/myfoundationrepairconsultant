Reinforcing steel is often described as if it prevented cracks. It does not. It changes how concrete carries tension after a crack has formed.

This page explains what rebar and tendons are for, what they cannot do, and why “there is steel in it” is not a complete answer.

What the steel is doing

Concrete is strong in compression. Steel is strong in tension. Together they make a member that can take bending. After concrete cracks in tension, the steel holds the section together and keeps crack widths smaller than they would be in plain concrete.

That is a performance role. It is not a promise of a crack-free slab.

What homeowners usually hear

  • It has rebar, so it should not crack.
  • Post-tension means the slab cannot move.
  • If it cracked, the steel failed.

A reinforced slab can crack and still be doing what it was designed to do.

What reinforcing steel does not do

  • It does not stop soil from changing volume
  • It does not replace drainage
  • It does not make a slab independent of the ground it sits on
  • It does not mean every crack is cosmetic

When steel details start to matter

  • A crack is wide, offset, or still growing
  • A post-tension slab shows unusual warping
  • Someone proposes cutting or drilling without knowing where the steel is

A final note

This page is educational, not diagnostic. Steel is part of how the house carries load. It is not a warranty against the material or the soil doing what they do.
