Cosmetic and structural are two of the most loaded words in a foundation conversation. One sounds harmless. The other sounds like the house is coming apart. Neither word, by itself, tells you what to do.

This page separates location and function from severity, and why a “structural crack” is not automatically an emergency.

What the words actually describe

Cosmetic usually means the crack or gap is in a finish — paint, drywall tape, a slab surface — and is not changing how the house carries load.

Structural usually means the crack is in an element that is part of the load path: a foundation wall, a beam, a bearing wall, a footing. It describes where the crack is, not how bad the house is.

Why location and severity get collapsed into one word

“Structural” is a real, useful word for describing what kind of element a crack sits in. It becomes a problem when it silently starts doing a second job — implying severity, urgency, or a required repair — without anyone actually checking whether those things are true. A crack can be in a genuinely structural element and still be old, narrow, and stable. The location tells you what to watch. It doesn’t tell you what you’re seeing yet.

Why the label gets used as a closer

Once a crack is called structural, the next sentence is often a method. That skip is the problem. A structural location can still be old, narrow, and stable. A cosmetic crack can still be the first visible sign of movement that has not been measured.

Questions that matter more than the label

  • Is the crack in a finish or in a load-bearing element?
  • Has the width or offset changed?
  • Does anything else in the house move with it?
  • Is the pattern seasonal or one-way?

What neither word automatically means

  • Cosmetic does not mean “ignore it forever”
  • Structural does not mean “sign today”
  • Neither word identifies the soil
  • Neither word selects a repair method

How MFRC suggests homeowners think about it

Instead of asking “is this structural,” ask “where is it, and has it changed?” The first question sorts a crack into a category. The second one actually tells you something.
