Underpinning is a method. Monitoring is a decision to gather time. Those are not opposites. One of them is often the honest first step.

This page explains when watching a house is the more responsible move than installing piers on the first visit.

What monitoring actually is

Monitoring is dated photographs, simple width marks, and — when it is worth the cost — measured elevations. It answers one question: is the movement still happening?

A single afternoon cannot answer that question. A sales visit that treats every crack as active movement is skipping the question.

When monitoring is the better first move

  • Symptoms look old and have not changed
  • The pattern is seasonal and partly recovers
  • A moisture source was just corrected
  • The house still functions and no red-flag offsets are growing

When monitoring is not enough

  • Change is documented and one-way
  • A load-bearing element is opening or offsetting
  • Doors, floors, and exterior lines are moving together
  • A geotechnical or structural evaluation already showed an active mechanism

What underpinning does not replace

Piers transfer load. They do not automatically stop heave in an active zone, fix a leak, or rewrite a drainage problem. Installing them because a crack exists is a method in search of a cause.
