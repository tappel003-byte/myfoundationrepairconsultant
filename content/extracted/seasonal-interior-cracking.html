Interior cracks that open in winter and ease in summer are often humidity and framing, not a footing that failed in December.

What seasonal cracking usually looks like

  • Drywall at corners and ceiling lines
  • A door that sticks in heating season and frees up later
  • The same line returning after a paint repair

Winter heating dries indoor air, and dry air pulls moisture out of framing lumber. Wood that loses moisture shrinks, mostly across the grain, and that small dimensional change is enough to open a taped joint or rack a door frame. When humidity returns in warmer months, the wood takes moisture back and often swells close to its earlier size — which is why the same crack can close up again on its own.

What it does not automatically mean

  • The foundation dropped over the holidays
  • The crack will become structural if you wait
  • Piers will stop a seasonal finish crack

When season is the wrong explanation

  • The gap grows and does not recover
  • Floors, exterior lines, or masonry are moving with it
  • A moisture source under the house changed at the same time

How MFRC suggests homeowners think about it

Instead of asking "why did this crack show up in winter," ask "did it close back up when the seasons changed, or is it still there?" A crack that comes and goes with the heating season is describing humidity. A crack that stays open year-round is describing something that isn't recovering.
