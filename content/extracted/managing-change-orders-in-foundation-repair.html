Change orders are formal changes to the original construction agreement. In foundation repair, they are common—even on well-planned projects—because some conditions are not fully visible until work begins.

Understanding why change orders occur, what they should include, and when to pause and ask questions helps homeowners stay in control of both cost and scope.

Why Change Orders Happen

Most change orders in foundation repair fall into a few predictable categories:

  • Previously Hidden Conditions
    Once excavation or structural work begins, additional cracking, movement, or deterioration may become visible.
  • Soil and Site Conditions
    Unexpected soil behavior, groundwater, obstructions, or access limitations can require adjustments to the repair approach.
  • Scope Modifications
    Additional work may be requested by the homeowner, or modifications may be recommended based on field observations.
  • Regulatory or Compliance Requirements
    Occasionally, inspections or permitting requirements introduce changes that were not known at the start of construction.

Not all change orders indicate a problem. Some reflect new information that could not reasonably have been identified earlier.

What a Proper Change Order Should Include

A well-documented change order clearly explains what is changing and why. At minimum, it should include:

  • A clear description of the added or revised work
  • An itemized cost adjustment, including labor and materials
  • Any impact to the project schedule
  • Written approval before the work proceeds

If consultants or third-party professionals are involved, change orders are typically reviewed with them before approval.

Red Flags to Watch For

Certain patterns may indicate that a closer review is warranted:

  • Vague descriptions that do not clearly explain the work or justification
  • Cost increases without explanation or supporting detail
  • Frequent change orders, which may suggest incomplete planning or unclear scope

When change orders begin to accumulate, it is reasonable to pause and reassess how the project is being managed.

The Role of Ongoing Communication

Regular communication with the on-site supervisor or foreman helps reduce surprises. Brief, consistent check-ins allow homeowners to:

  • Understand daily progress
  • Identify potential issues early
  • Anticipate changes before they become formal change orders

Many change orders are easier to evaluate when homeowners are already aware of the conditions that led to them.

Managing Change Without Losing Control

Change orders are a normal part of foundation repair, but they should never feel automatic or confusing. Clear documentation, thoughtful review, and consistent communication help ensure that changes are justified and understood.
